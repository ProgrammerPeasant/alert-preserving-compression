\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Scope of the rules.} We handle rules that read one series.
Aggregations across series (\code{sum by}) couple the budgets of all
series in a group, so the encoder would have to see the group together.
Ratios need an interval enclosure, and equality tests and \code{topk}
admit no positive budget at their decision points.

\paragraph{Known rules.} The budget is computed for a fixed rule set. A
rule added later reads a reconstruction that was optimised for other
rules, and only the optional pointwise cap bounds its error. The series
can be compressed again from the original as long as the original is kept,
for example until compaction.

\paragraph{Lookahead.} A sample's budget depends on evaluations up to one
window after it, so the encoder must buffer the longest window. This works
during compaction or in a buffering proxy, but not on a write path that
must add no latency.

\paragraph{Data.} Our real data are 13 series from two public demo
servers. The deployed thresholds never fire there, so we recalibrated the
thresholds to quantiles. An evaluation on production traces with more
rules, more incidents and gaps in the data is still needed.

\paragraph{Relaxations.} Requiring the same \textsf{Pending} periods makes
the criterion strict. If only firings had to be preserved, or firing times
up to $\delta$, the budget could exceed the margin on short condition
flips, since \code{for} filters them out. The analysis would then have to
reason about the automaton instead of single evaluations. The minimum in
\eqref{eq:budget} gives a feasible budget but not an optimal one. For
linear aggregates, the optimal split of a margin among the samples of a
window is a knapsack-like problem.

\section{Conclusion}

With alert decisions as the correctness criterion, lossy compression of
monitoring data has a sound per-sample error budget that can be computed
in linear time. The aggregates are monotone, so the budget is one-sided.
A coder that keeps its prediction whenever it is still admissible turns
this into an output 2.4--2.5 times smaller than with the symmetric
Lipschitz box. On real Prometheus series the codec needs from a few
tenths of a bit to a few bits per value, and no alert decision changes.
