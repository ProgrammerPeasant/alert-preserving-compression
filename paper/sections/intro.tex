\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Monitoring systems such as Prometheus~\cite{prometheus} store millions of
floating-point time series, and most of the stored bytes are never read by a
human. They are read by alerting rules: threshold tests over windowed
aggregates such as \code{avg\_over\_time(x[5m]) > 0.8 for 10m} or
\code{rate(requests[5m]) < 20}. Lossless time-series compressors in the
Gorilla family~\cite{pelkonen2015gorilla} spend 20--55 bits per value on
typical gauges, and error-bounded lossy compressors such as
SZ3~\cite{liang2023sz3} trade a uniform pointwise bound $\eps$ for size. A
uniform bound is the wrong currency for alerting: far from a threshold almost
any error is harmless, while near a threshold an error smaller than the
measurement noise can create or suppress an alert. Choosing $\eps$ by hand
either wastes space or silently changes which alerts fire.

We take the decisions themselves as the correctness criterion. A lossy
compressor is \emph{alert-preserving} for a rule set if, for every rule,
the alert state trajectory (\code{Inactive}/\code{Pending}/\code{Firing} at
every evaluation) computed on the reconstruction equals the one computed on
the original. Compression is then free to discard everything the rules
cannot observe.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A formal model of PromQL threshold alerts over one series
    (left-open windows, \code{for}/\code{keep\_firing\_for} automaton,
    Prometheus' extrapolated \code{rate}) and decision preservation as a
    correctness criterion, including \emph{phase robustness}: preservation for
    every evaluation interval and phase, without knowing the window lengths in
    samples (\cref{sec:model}).
  \item A per-sample error budget derived from the rules: the minimum, over
    every evaluation whose window contains the sample, of the evaluation's
    margin divided by a Lipschitz constant of its aggregate
    (\cref{thm:budget}). Because every aggregate except \code{rate} is
    monotone, the budget splits into independent downward and upward
    tolerances (\cref{cor:onesided}); for \code{rate} we characterise counter
    resets as discontinuities and show that only the window ends carry a
    budget under an order-preserving quantizer (\cref{prop:rate}). The
    budget is computed in $O(n)$ per rule.
  \item A codec that turns the asymmetric budget into bits: closed-loop DPCM
    on power-of-two grids with a \emph{lazy} step choice, adaptive binary range
    coding, and a verify-and-repair loop against a reference evaluator that
    makes the guarantee end to end in floating point. An optimistic variant
    starts from a multiple of the analytic budget and repairs only the
    evaluations that flip, with the analytic budget as the floor
    (\cref{sec:codec}).
  \item Machine-checked bit-precise proofs (Kani~\cite{vanhattum2022kani})
    of the floating-point budget for \code{last}, \code{min\_over\_time} and
    \code{max\_over\_time} and of codec invariants (\cref{sec:verification}).
  \item An evaluation on synthetic series and on 13 real series from the
    public Prometheus demo servers with their upstream alert rules
    (7- and 30-day snapshots, released with the code), against uniform
    error bounds and Compression Safeguards~\cite{tyree2026safeguards}, the
    closest related system, with an ablation of every codec component
    (\cref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Results in brief.} No rule-aware output changed a single alert
state, over 1\,864 (7-day) and 7\,191 (30-day) firings on real data. With
thresholds that fire, the codec needs about 1--1.5 bits per value on real
series, $4$--$6.5\times$ less than a uniform bound tuned by hand on the
same data (which has no guarantee), and it is smaller than Compression
Safeguards over SZ3 on 19 of 26 real rule variants and on every synthetic
one. The ablation attributes a $2.3$--$2.5\times$ reduction to the
combination of the one-sided budget with the lazy step choice.
