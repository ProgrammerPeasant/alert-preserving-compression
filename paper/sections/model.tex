\section{Model and correctness criterion}
\label{sec:model}

We define series, compressors, rules and alert states, and then the
correctness criterion. The definitions do not assume knowledge of PromQL;
\cref{tab:promql} lists the Prometheus counterpart of each object.

\subsection{Series and compressors}

\begin{definition}[Series]
\label{def:series}
A \emph{series} of length $n$ is a finite sequence of samples
$X = ((s_i, x_i))_{i=1}^{n}$, where $s_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the timestamp
of sample $i$ in milliseconds, $s_1 < s_2 < \dots < s_n$, and
$x_i \in \mathbb{R}$ is its value. We write $I = \{1, \dots, n\}$ for the
index set.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Value-only compressor]
\label{def:compressor}
A \emph{compressor} is a pair of maps: an encoder that turns a series into
a bit string and a decoder that turns the bit string back into a series. It
is \emph{value-only} if the decoded series has the same timestamps,
$Y = ((s_i, y_i))_{i=1}^{n}$; only the values may change. We write
$C(X) = Y$ for the composition of encoder and decoder and
$e_i = y_i - x_i$ for the pointwise error. $C$ is \emph{lossless} on $X$ if
$e_i = 0$ for all $i \in I$.
\end{definition}

Timestamps are compressed losslessly by existing delta-of-delta
schemes~\cite{pelkonen2015gorilla} and are out of scope; every compressor in
this paper is value-only.

Monitoring systems distinguish metric types by how their values may evolve.
Prometheus has gauges, counters, histograms and summaries; the classic
forms of the last two are stored as groups of gauge and counter
series~\cite{prometheus}, so two types suffice for our analysis.

\begin{definition}[Gauges and counters]
\label{def:kinds}
A series $X$ is a \emph{gauge} if its values are arbitrary reals: they
measure a current level that may go up and down (load average, memory in
use, temperature). It is a \emph{counter} if its values are non-negative
and count events cumulatively since the start of the monitored process
(requests served, bytes sent), so that they never decrease except at a
\emph{reset}, an index $i > 1$ with $x_i < x_{i-1}$, where the process
restarted and the count began again from zero.
\end{definition}

The kind is metadata of the series (the \code{TYPE} line of the exposition
format), not something inferred from the values. Counters are used through
their rate of increase (the aggregate $\mathrm{rate}$ below), gauges
through their level.

\subsection{Alerting rules}

\begin{definition}[Threshold rule]
\label{def:rule}
A \emph{threshold rule} is a tuple
\[
  R = (F,\ w,\ \bowtie,\ \theta,\ d_{\mathrm{hold}},\ d_{\mathrm{keep}}),
\]
whose components are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $F$, the \emph{aggregate}: a function that maps a finite non-empty
    sequence of samples to a real number. We consider
    $F \in \{\mathrm{last}, \mathrm{avg}, \mathrm{sum}, \mathrm{min},
    \mathrm{max}, \mathrm{rate}\}$. For values $v_1, \dots, v_m$,
    $\mathrm{last} = v_m$, $\mathrm{avg} = \frac1m \sum_j v_j$,
    $\mathrm{sum} = \sum_j v_j$, and $\mathrm{min}$, $\mathrm{max}$ are the
    extrema; $\mathrm{rate}$ is the per-second increase of a counter, which
    also depends on the timestamps (\cref{sec:budget-rate});
  \item $w > 0$, the \emph{range}: the length of the time window the
    aggregate reads, in milliseconds;
  \item ${\bowtie} \in \{>, \ge, <, \le\}$, the \emph{comparison};
  \item $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, the \emph{threshold};
  \item $d_{\mathrm{hold}} \ge 0$, the \emph{hold duration}: how long the
    condition must stay true before the alert fires;
  \item $d_{\mathrm{keep}} \ge 0$, the \emph{keep duration}: how long a
    firing alert keeps firing after the condition has become false.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For example, the Prometheus alerting rule
\begin{center}
\code{expr: avg\_over\_time(node\_load1[5m]) > 2}\qquad \code{for: 10m}
\end{center}
is the tuple $R = (\mathrm{avg},\ 5\,\mathrm{min},\ {>},\ 2,\
10\,\mathrm{min},\ 0)$. \Cref{tab:promql} lists the correspondence for
every component.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Rules are evaluated at instants of an
\emph{evaluation grid} $T = (t_1 < t_2 < \dots)$; in Prometheus the grid is
fixed by the evaluation interval and an unknown phase. At an instant $t$ the
rule reads the samples whose index lies in its \emph{support}
\[
  S_R(t) = \{\, i \in I : t - w < s_i \le t \,\}.
\]
The window is open on the left, as range selectors are in Prometheus~3. For
$F = \mathrm{last}$ the expression has no range; the support is the single
latest sample within the lookback delta, $S_R(t) = \{\max\{ i : t - \lambda <
s_i \le t \}\}$ with $\lambda = 5$\,min, or $\emptyset$ if there is none.
Given the support, the rule computes two quantities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{aggregate value}
    $f_R(X)(t) = F\bigl((s_i, x_i)_{i \in S_R(t)}\bigr)$, defined when
    $S_R(t) \ne \emptyset$;
  \item the \emph{condition}
    $c_R(X)(t) = \bigl[\, S_R(t) \ne \emptyset \ \wedge\ f_R(X)(t) \bowtie
    \theta \,\bigr] \in \{0, 1\}$.
\end{itemize}
An empty support makes the condition false, as Prometheus returns no sample
then.

\subsection{Alert states}

Whether an alert fires also depends on the hold and keep durations, so the
rule has to remember past conditions. Prometheus keeps this memory in a
small state machine (\code{rules/alerting.go}~\cite{promsrc}); we restate it as an automaton.

\begin{definition}[Alert automaton]
\label{def:automaton}
The automaton $M_R$ has states $Q = \{\mathsf{Inactive}, \mathsf{Pending},
\mathsf{Firing}\}$ and two timers, the activation time $a$ and the keep
start $b \in \mathbb{Z} \cup \{\bot\}$. It starts in $\mathsf{Inactive}$
with $b = \bot$ and reads pairs $(t_j, c_j)$, where $c_j = c_R(X)(t_j)$. At
each step $j = 1, 2, \dots$ it applies, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item if $c_j = 1$: set $b \gets \bot$; if the state is $\mathsf{Inactive}$,
    move to $\mathsf{Pending}$ and set $a \gets t_j$;
  \item if $c_j = 0$: if the state is $\mathsf{Firing}$ and
    $d_{\mathrm{keep}} > 0$, set $b \gets t_j$ when $b = \bot$, and stay in
    $\mathsf{Firing}$ while $t_j - b < d_{\mathrm{keep}}$; otherwise move to
    $\mathsf{Inactive}$ and set $b \gets \bot$;
  \item if the state is $\mathsf{Pending}$ and
    $t_j - a \ge d_{\mathrm{hold}}$, move to $\mathsf{Firing}$.
\end{enumerate}
The state after step $j$ is $q_j$. The \emph{alert state trajectory} of $R$
on $X$ over $T$ is $D_R(X) = (q_1, q_2, \dots) \in Q^{|T|}$.
\end{definition}

With $d_{\mathrm{hold}} = 0$ step~3 fires on the first true condition, so
$\mathsf{Pending}$ is never observed; with $d_{\mathrm{keep}} = 0$ a false
condition always resets the alert.

\subsection{Correctness criterion}

\begin{definition}[Decision preservation]
\label{def:preservation}
Let $\R$ be a finite set of rules. A compressor $C$ is
\emph{decision-preserving} for $\R$ on $X$ over a grid $T$ if
$D_R(C(X)) = D_R(X)$ for every $R \in \R$. It is \emph{phase-robust} if it
is decision-preserving over every grid $T$.
\end{definition}

The criterion compares whole trajectories. It therefore also requires
the same $\mathsf{Pending}$ periods, which is stricter than requiring the
same firings. Relaxations (firing times within $\delta$, firings only)
admit larger budgets and are left for future work
(\cref{sec:conclusion}).

The two lemmas below reduce decision preservation to preserving the
condition at finitely many instants.

\begin{lemma}[Automaton]
\label{lem:automaton}
If $c_R(X)(t) = c_R(Y)(t)$ for all $t \in T$, then $D_R(X) = D_R(Y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$M_R$ reads only the instants $t_j$ and the conditions $c_j$, which coincide
for $X$ and $Y$; its transitions are deterministic.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Phase robustness]
\label{lem:phase}
Let $F \ne \mathrm{rate}$, so that $f_R(X)(t)$ depends on $t$ only through
the support $S_R(t)$. Let
$E_R = \{s_i : i \in I\} \cup \{s_i + w : i \in I\}$ (with $\lambda$ in
place of $w$ for $F = \mathrm{last}$). Then $S_R(t)$, and hence $c_R(X)(t)$
and $c_R(Y)(t)$, is constant on every interval between consecutive points
of $E_R$, and $S_R(t) = \emptyset$ before the first point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By definition $i \in S_R(t) \iff s_i \le t < s_i + w$, so the membership of
$i$ changes only at $t = s_i$ or $t = s_i + w$. For $\mathrm{last}$ the
support is the largest index in the same kind of window with $\lambda$
in place of $w$, which changes only at the same kind of points.
\end{proof}

Call the points of $P_R = E_R$ the \emph{protected instants} of $R$; there
are at most $2n$ of them. By \cref{lem:phase}, every instant $t$ has the
same condition as some protected instant (the last one not after $t$), for
both $X$ and $Y$. With \cref{lem:automaton} this gives:

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:protected}
If $c_R(C(X))(t) = c_R(X)(t)$ for every $R \in \R$ with
$F \ne \mathrm{rate}$ and every $t \in P_R$, then $C$ is phase-robust for
these rules.
\end{corollary}

The encoder therefore needs the rule's range $w$. It does not need the
evaluation interval, the phase, the scrape interval or the number of
samples per window, so the guarantee also covers missed scrapes and
irregular timestamps. The
$\mathrm{rate}$ aggregate depends on $t$ continuously through its
extrapolation to the window edges (\cref{sec:budget-rate}); for it the
conditions are preserved on the deployed grid $T$ only, $P_R = T$.
